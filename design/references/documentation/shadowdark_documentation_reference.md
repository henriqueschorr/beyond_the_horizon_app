# Codex Presentation & UX Report: *Shadowdark RPG*
*An exhaustive analysis of Information Architecture, Digest-Sized Zero-Page-Turn Spreads, High-Contrast Black & White Inks, and Cognitive Ergonomics in Kelsey Dionne's Multi-Award-Winning Dungeon Crawler.*

---

## Executive Summary

*Shadowdark RPG* (designed by **Kelsey Dionne**, published by **The Arcane Library**) achieved one of the most historic sweeps in tabletop history at the **2024 ENNIE Awards**, taking home four Gold awards:
- 🏆 **Product of the Year (Gold)**
- 🏆 **Best Game (Gold)**
- 🏆 **Best Layout and Design (Gold)**
- 🏆 **Best Rules (Gold)**

*Shadowdark* is celebrated across the RPG industry as the modern gold standard of **Cognitive Ergonomics**. Dionne designed the book with one non-negotiable directive: **a Game Master running a game in real-time under low light should never, under any circumstances, have to flip a page to resolve an encounter, look up a monster, or cast a spell.**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    THE CORE DESIGN AXIOMS OF SHADOWDARK                 │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. The Zero-Turn Spread Rule: Every rule, spell list, class, encounter  │
│    table, and monster statblock is completely contained on a single     │
│    page or a single 2-page spread. Nothing overflows across a turn.     │
│ 2. High-Contrast Black & White Inks: Rejects color printing entirely.   │

│ 3. Distressed Woodcut Banners: Inverted black headers with textured     │
```

## 1. Information Architecture & Macro-Organization

Spanning 183 pages in a compact **Digest Size (5.5 x 8.5 inches / A5 equivalent)**, *Shadowdark* eliminates extraneous lore encyclopedias, focusing entirely on playable dungeon dungeon-crawling utility:

```
[ FRONT INSIDE COVER ] ──> P.3:   Immediate Table Reference (DC, Dying, Distance)
[ CHAPTER I: HEROES  ] ──> P.6-46: Characters, Classes, Talents, Gear
[ CHAPTER II: MAGIC  ] ──> P.47-86: Spellcasting Rules, Spell Lists, Spell Tiers 1-5
[ CHAPTER III: PLAY  ] ──> P.87-114: Crawling, Darkness, Hazards, Combat, Torches
[ CHAPTER IV: GM     ] ──> P.115-144: Running the Game, Dungeon Generation, Traps
[ CHAPTER V: MONSTERS] ──> P.145-180: Bestiary (Each Monster Fits in 1/4 or 1/2 Page)
[ INDEX & TABLES     ] ──> P.181-183: Quick Table Index
```

### A. The Front-Cover Loading Trick (P.3)
Unlike standard books that waste the first 5 to 10 pages on copyright fine-print, prefaces, and dedications, *Shadowdark* places the **most critical mechanical reference tables right on Page 3 (inside front cover)**:
- `Difficulty Class` (Easy DC 9, Normal DC 12, Hard DC 15, Extreme DC 18)
- `Distance` (Close = 5ft, Near = up to 30ft, Far = within sight)
- `Movement` (Swimming, Falling, Moving Through)
- `Morale` (When enemies flee)
- `Dying & Death Timer` (1d4 + CON rounds, DC 15 INT stabilize check)
- `Spell Concentration` (Success, Failure, Distraction)

A GM holding the book never has to search through Chapter III during life-or-death moments.

### A. The Digest-Size Ergonomic Advantage
- The smaller 5.5 x 8.5 inch page format fits comfortably in one hand and leaves ample space on crowded gaming tables covered with dice trays, character sheets, and miniatures.
- Reading distance is naturally shorter, allowing for clean 1-column or tight 2-column layouts that prevent the "eye-tracking fatigue" common in large 8.5 x 11 three-column tomes.

### B. Chapter Thumb Edge Tabs
- The outer edge of every page carries a **solid grey-and-black rectangular thumb tab**.
- As chapters progress (*Heroes* → *Magic* → *Gameplay* → *Monsters*), the tab shifts downward along the outer margin, allowing instant tactile indexing with a quick flick of the thumb.

## 3. Typographic Identity: The Blackletter / Modernist Dialogue

*Shadowdark*'s typography creates a powerful psychological dialect: the **archaic gothic dread of medieval catacombs** contrasted with the **crisp, surgical readability of modern minimalist design**.

### A. Font Breakdown

| Role | Font Style / Family | Visual Characteristic | Usage & Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Macro Titles & Themes** | **Gothic Blackletter / German Fraktur** | Dense, fractured, medieval quill calligraphy | Chapter title splashes (`Difficulty Class`, `Magic`, `Monsters`). |
| **Inverted Banner Titles** | **Geometric Sans (All Caps Bold)** | Clean, modern, high-contrast white-on-black | Item headers (`EGG OF THE COCKATRICE`, `GENIE LAMP`, `GOBLIN BOMB`). |
| **Section & Spell Names** | **Modern Bold Sans (Futura / Century Gothic)**| Bold uppercase and small caps | Spell list categories (`TIER 1`, `TIER 2`), Monster names. |
| **Core Body Copy** | **Geometric Sans (Montserrat / Proxima Nova)**| Regular, 9.5pt on 13.5pt leading | Crisp, sans-serif body text; zero serif clutter. |
| **Bold Semantic Lead-Ins** | **Heavy Bold Sans** | Bold sentence starter followed by period | `Benefit.`, `Personality.`, `DC 15.`, `Stabilize.`, `Damage.`. |
| **Atmospheric Flavour** | **Italic Sans / Minion Italic** | Elegant, tilted serif or sans-serif | In-world character quotes, sensory item flavor text. |

### B. The Keyword-First Formatting Pattern
Dionne never buries mechanical resolution in prose. Notice how magic items are formatted on P.247:

```
[ WOODCUT BANNER: GENIE LAMP ]
A tarnished, brass oil lamp.
Benefit. Rubbing the lamp causes its resident djinni (50% chance) or efreeti (50% chance) to emerge...
```

The user reads:
1. **Title** (Woodcut banner: *What is this?*)
2. **Sensory Description** (*What does it look and feel like?* — 1 short sentence)
3. **Benefit / Trigger** (*What does it do in game terms?* — preceded by bold `Benefit.`)

## 5. The 5-Second Monster Statblock

One of *Shadowdark*'s greatest ergonomic breakthroughs is its monster statblock architecture. Traditional games (D&D 5e, Pathfinder) feature monster statblocks that occupy half a page to a full page, cluttered with redundant saving throws, passive skills, and paragraphs of spell descriptions.

*Shadowdark* condenses every monster into a **tiny, modular card** that can be absorbed in under 5 seconds:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ GOBLIN                                                 │
│ Small, lawful evil                                     │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ AC 12, HP 4, ATK 1 dagger (+1) or 1 shortbow (+1),     │
│ MV near, S -1, D +1, C +0, I -1, W +0, Ch -1, AL C     │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Keen Senses. Goblins cannot be surprised.              │
│ Sneaky. +2 to stealth checks.                          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Line 1**: Name, Size, Alignment.
- **Line 2**: AC, HP, Attacks, Movement, Ability Modifiers.
- **Lines 3-4**: 1 to 2 bulleted special abilities.
- **Result**: Four to six different monsters fit comfortably on a single digest page, eliminating all monster book-keeping.

## 6. Key Takeaways & Actionable Codex UX Strategies

1. **The Inside-Cover "Golden Real Estate" Rule**:
   - In a web or mobile application, never waste the default initial view on marketing banners or lengthy prefaces. Put the core operational dials (difficulty targets, combat flow, status conditions) immediately at the user's fingertips.
2. **Digest Chunking (The Single-Viewport Principle)**:
   - Restrict units of information to single viewport heights. When an item, monster, or spell requires scrolling, its usability plummets during fast-paced operational sessions.
3. **High-Contrast Monochromatic Hierarchy**:
   - Color is a luxury, not a crutch. If a user interface cannot be navigated effortlessly in pure black and white, adding colors will only create visual noise.
4. **Keyword-First Semantics**:
   - Structure every operational description so the key action verb or category (`Trigger:`, `Benefit:`, `Failure:`, `Duration:`) is the very first bolded word.
